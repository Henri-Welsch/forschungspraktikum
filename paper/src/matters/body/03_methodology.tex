\section{Methodology: Time-Dependent Functional RoA}
\label{sec:methodology}

To quantify the impact of cascading infrastructure failures on urban resilience, we extend the static Reliability of Accessibility (RoA) index into a dynamic, time-dependent framework. This methodology incorporates the temporal progression of flood events, infrastructure coupling via road-network constraints, and the internal resilience of facilities managed through a finite state machine.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework of RoA(t)}
\label{subsec:mathematical-framework-of-roa-t}

\subsubsection{Time Deterministic RoA:}
While the core mathematical structure of the standard RoA index is preserved, accessibility is dynamically modified downstream by facility functionality, which directly governs destination reachability and the resulting RoA calculation. We operationalize this metric into a time-dependent index $\text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t)$ evaluated at each simulation hour $t$ within a disaster horizon $T$:

\begin{equation}
    \text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \left( a_{\mathcal{C},j} \cdot \frac{c(s_{j},d_{j}, t_0)}{c(s_j, d_j, t)} \right)
\end{equation}

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    $\text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t)$ & Functional area-wide accessibility index at time $t$. \\
    $\mathcal{C}$ / $m$ & Set ($\mathcal{C}$) and count ($m$) of origin-destination POI connections. \\
    $a_{\mathcal{C},j}$ & Weighting factor of routs j for POI $\mathcal{C}$ subject to $\sum_{j=1}^{m} a_{\mathcal{C},j} = 1$. \\
    $c(s_j, d_j, t_0)$ & Travel cost from origin $s_j$ to destination $d_j$ at initial time $t_0$. \\
    $c(s_j, d_j, t)$ & Travel cost from origin $s_j$ to destination $d_j$ at simulation time $t$.
\end{tabularx}

\subsubsection{Time-Integrated RoA over $T$}
Because $\text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t)$ is evaluated continuously over the course of the simulation rather than as a static snapshot, total resilience over the crisis period is captured by the time-integrated RoA ($\text{RoA}_{\text{Int}}$):

\begin{equation}
    \text{RoA}_{\text{Int}}(\mathcal{C}) = \frac{1}{t_{\text{end}} - t_{\text{start}}} \int_{t_{\text{start}}}^{t_{\text{end}}} \text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t) \, dt
\end{equation}

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    $\text{RoA}_{\text{Int}}(\mathcal{C})$ & Cumulative time-integrated resilience index over the disaster horizon. \\
    $\mathcal{C}$ & Set of evaluated origin-destination POI connections. \\
    $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ & Analysis period from the start ($t_{\text{start}}$) to the end ($t_{\text{end}}$) of the disaster. \\
    $\frac{1}{t_{\text{end}} - t_{\text{start}}}$ & Normalizing the area under the resilience curve to $[0, 1]$. \\
    $\text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}, t)$ & Time-dependent accessibility index of connections $\mathcal{C}$ at time $t$. \\
    $dt$ & Differential element for integration over time $t$.
\end{tabularx}

\subsection{POI Health: Finite State Machine and State Transitions}
\label{subsec:poi-health-finite-state-machine}

While physical road availability is a necessary condition for accessibility, it is insufficient to guarantee operational functionality.
Facilities such as hospitals and fire stations depend heavily on external utilities (power and water).
When utility grid connections fail, internal backup systems allow temporary operation until reserves deplete.
These states and transitions need to be governed by a Finite State Machine (FSM).

\subsubsection{FSM States:}
To capture this dynamic functionality, we model each POI $i$ using a four-state Finite State Machine (FSM). At any time step $t$, the state of a facility is defined as $\sigma_i(t)$, which determines an operational binary indicator $f_i(t) \in \{0, 1\}$ indicating whether a facility can actively accept traffic and deliver service:

\noindent
\begin{minipage}{0.40\textwidth}
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma_i(t) \in \left\{ \begin{aligned}
                                    &\text{Oper}, \text{Depl.}, \\
                                    &\text{Dead}, \text{Rebo.}
        \end{aligned} \right\}
    \end{equation}
\end{minipage}%
\begin{minipage}{0.60\textwidth}
    \begin{equation}
        f_i(t) = \begin{cases}
                     1 & \text{if } \sigma_i(t) \in \{\text{Oper.}, \text{Depl.}\} \\
                     0 & \text{if } \sigma_i(t) \in \{\text{Dead}, \text{Rebo.}\}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{7mm}

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    Operational & All external utility connections are active and reserve buffers recharge; the facility is fully functional ($f_i(t) = 1$). \\
    Depleting & At least one utility connection has failed while internal reserves bridge the outage; the facility remains functional ($f_i(t) = 1$). \\
    Dead & At least one reserve buffer is fully exhausted or the facility site is directly flooded; the facility is non-functional ($f_i(t) = 0$). \\
    Rebooting & All utility supply are restored or buffer reserves are above thresholds; the facility is non-functional ($f_i(t) = 0$).
\end{tabularx}

\subsubsection{FSM State Transitions:}
State transitions are governed by resource availability, continuous flood exposure, and hysteresis rules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operational Transitions ($\sigma_i(t) = \text{Operational}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Operational $\to$ Depleting:} Triggered when any grid connection fails ($\exists r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\} : u_{i,r}(t) = 0$), provided backup reserves exist ($b_{i,r}(t) > 0$).
        \item \textbf{Operational $\to$ Dead:} Direct flood override. Triggered immediately if the facility site is submerged ($\mathbf{p}_i \in \text{FloodPolygon}(t)$).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Depleting Transitions ($\sigma_i(t) = \text{Depleting}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Depleting $\to$ Operational:} Triggered if full utility connectivity is restored ($\forall r : u_{i,r}(t) = 1$) before any buffer is exhausted ($\forall r : b_{i,r}(t) > 0$).
        \item \textbf{Depleting $\to$ Dead:} Triggered if at least one resource buffer drops to zero ($\exists r : b_{i,r}(t) = 0$) OR if the site is submerged ($\mathbf{p}_i \in \text{FloodPolygon}(t)$).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Dead Transitions ($\sigma_i(t) = \text{Dead}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Dead $\to$ Rebooting:} Triggered when unflooded ($\mathbf{p}_i \notin \text{FloodPolygon}(t)$) and restart viability is met for both $r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}$: $u_{i,r}(t) = 1 \lor b_{i,r}(t) > \theta \cdot B_r$ (where $\theta = 0.15$).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rebooting Transitions ($\sigma_i(t) = \text{Rebooting}$):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Rebooting $\to$ Operational:} When delay completes ($t_{\text{reboot}} \ge T_{\text{delay}}$) and all grid connections are online ($\forall r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}, \, u_{i,r}(t) = 1$).
        \item \textbf{Rebooting $\to$ Depleting:} When delay completes ($t_{\text{reboot}} \ge T_{\text{delay}}$) while a partial grid outage persists ($\exists r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\} : u_{i,r}(t) = 0$).
        \item \textbf{Rebooting $\to$ Dead:} Triggered if submerged ($\mathbf{p}_i \in \text{FloodPolygon}(t)$) OR if restart viability is lost for any resource line ($\exists r : u_{i,r}(t) = 0 \land b_{i,r}(t) \le \theta \cdot B_r$, where $\theta = 0.15$).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{FSM Buffer Dynamics and Hysteresis:}
Each POI maintains independent reserve buffers $b_{i,r}(t)$ for each resource $r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}$ with maximum capacity $B_r$. During grid outages ($u_{i,r}(t) = 0$), reserves deplete at rate $\lambda_r$. When grid connectivity is restored ($u_{i,r}(t) = 1$), reserves recharge at rate $\mu_r$:

\begin{equation}
    b_{i,r}(t + \Delta t) = \begin{cases}

                                \max\left(0, \, b_{i,r}(t) - \lambda_r \cdot \Delta t\right) & \text{if } u_{i,r}(t) = 0 \land f_i(t) = 1 \\
                                \min\left(B_r, \, b_{i,r}(t) + \mu_r \cdot \Delta t\right) & \text{if } u_{i,r}(t) = 1 \land f_i(t) = 1
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    $b_{i,r}(t), \, B_r$ & Current reserve level and maximum capacity for resource $r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}$ at POI $i$. \\
    $\lambda_r, \, \mu_r$ & Depletion and recharge rates per unit time. \\
    $\Delta t$ & Simulation timestep duration. \\
    $u_{i,r}(t), \, f_i(t)$ & Binary grid connection status and site viability indicator ($\in \{0, 1\}$). \\
    $\max, \min$ & Clamping bounds enforcing physical reserve limits $[0, B_r]$.
\end{tabularx}

\subsection{Infrastructure Coupling via NCNN}
\label{subsec:infrastructure-coupling-via-ncnn}

Critical utility networks (power and water) physically follow street corridors underground.
To capture topological barriers without assuming unconstrained Euclidean connectivity, utility dependencies are established using Network-Constrained Nearest-Neighbor (NCNN) mapping over an edge-weighted undirected street graph $G = (V, E)$.
Each POI $i$ is mapped to its topologically closest supply asset $s^*(i, r)$ for resource type $r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}$ via a multi-source shortest-path search:

\begin{equation}
    s^*(i, r) = \arg\min_{s \in \mathcal{I}_r} \text{dist}_G\left(\pi(\mathbf{p}_i), \pi(s)\right)
\end{equation}

\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l X@{}}
    $s^*(i, r)$ & Assigned supply asset of resource type $r$ serving facility $i$. \\
    $\mathcal{I}_r$ & Set of active supply asset nodes for resource $r \in \{\text{power}, \text{water}\}$. \\
    $\text{dist}_G(\cdot, \cdot)$ & Shortest network path distance over street graph $G = (V, E)$. \\
    $\pi(\mathbf{p}_i), \pi(s)$ & Graph snapping operator projecting spatial coordinates of POI $i$ and asset $s$ onto $V$.
\end{tabularx}

\vspace{7mm} \noindent
This topological assignment governs the dynamic grid status $u_{i,r}(t)$ within the facility FSM:

\begin{equation}
    u_{i,r}(t) = \begin{cases}
                     0 & \text{if } \mathbf{p}_{s^*(i,r)} \in \text{FloodPolygon}(t) \\
                     1 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\noindent
If the assigned supply asset $s^*(i, r)$ is inundated, the utility connection is severed ($u_{i,r}(t) = 0$), initiating buffer depletion ($b_{i,r}$) in the POI's state machine.
