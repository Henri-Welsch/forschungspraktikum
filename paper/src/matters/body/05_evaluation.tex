\section{Ergebnisse und Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

% - Evaluationmetrics
% - Vergleich der Level A, B und C
% - Sensitivitätsanalyse
% - Überprüfung der Hypothesen

The resilience of Trier's critical infrastructure is evaluated across four tiers of increasing complexity. These tiers allow us to isolate the impact of road disruptions, single-utility failures, and compound cascading failures.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Evaluation Framework}
\label{subsec:multi-tier-evaluation-framework}

\subsubsection{Level A: Road Baseline}
\label{subsubsec:level-a-road-baseline}
This level serves as the baseline, representing the impact of road flooding only. Facility functionality is assumed to be perfect (infinite utility buffers). $\text{RoA}(t)$ reflects only the increase in travel distance caused by inundated street segments.

\subsubsection{Level B1: Power Cascade}
\label{subsubsec:level-b1-power-cascade}
Level B1 introduces the dependency on the power grid. Facilities operate with limited power buffers ($72\,\text{h}$ for hospitals, $24\,\text{h}$ for fire stations). If the assigned power substation is flooded, the facility begins depleting its reserve. Water supply is assumed to be infinite.

\subsubsection{Level B2: Water Cascade}
\label{subsubsec:level-b2-water-cascade}
Symmetric to B1, this tier models the impact of water outages. Hospitals and fire stations have water reserves of $48\,\text{h}$ and $12\,\text{h}$, respectively. Power is considered unconstrained.

\subsubsection{Level C: Compound Failure}
\label{subsubsec:level-c-compound-failure}
Level C represents the most realistic scenario, where road disruptions, power cascades, and water cascades act simultaneously. A facility fails if \textit{either} resource is exhausted or its own site is flooded. This level also fully exercises the FSM's hysteresis and reboot delay logic.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:evaluation-metrics}

The primary metric is the time-integrated RoA ($\text{RoA}_{\text{Int}}$), which provides a single percentage value of urban resilience. Additionally, we evaluate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acute Nadir}: The minimum $\text{RoA}(t)$ reached during the flood peak.
    \item \textbf{Recovery Rate}: The time taken for $\text{RoA}$ to return to pre-event levels after the flood recedes.
    \item \textbf{Facility Downtime}: The total duration each POI spent in the \textbf{Dead} or \textbf{Rebooting} state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Observed Resilience Trends}
\label{subsec:observed-resilience-trends}

Initial simulation results demonstrate a non-linear decrease in accessibility as tiers progress. While Level A shows resilience dominated by the Moselle's geographic footprint, Levels B1 and B2 reveal vulnerabilities in facilities far from the flood zone that are coupled to vulnerable utility nodes. Level C exhibits a ``compounding effect'' where the total service loss exceeds the sum of individual power and water outages, particularly during the recession phase when facilities remain in the \textbf{Rebooting} state despite utility restoration.
