\section{Results and Evaluation}
\label{sec:results-and-evaluation}

The following section presents the quantitative and spatial results of the multi-tier simulation across the 336-hour flood horizon. We evaluate the impact of infrastructure coupling on the integrated robustness of accessibility ($RoA_{\text{Int}}$) and verify the research hypotheses regarding functional gaps and recovery lag.

\subsection{Scope and Methodological Baseline}
\label{subsec:scope-and-baseline}

A critical finding of our validation phase is that the municipal Level A baseline (Road Baseline) yields an integrated $RoA_{\text{Int}} = 88.31\,\%$ with a nadir of $65.50\,\%$.
While this differs from the $70.1\,\%$ benchmark reported by Kaub et al.~\cite{kaub2024}, it is a direct consequence of our more focused municipal scope.

The original study included the broader Trier region, capturing $44$ fire stations and a road network of $26,853$ nodes, providing significant decentralized redundancy via suburban volunteer departments.
In contrast, our study focuses strictly on the municipal boundary of Trier ($\sim 117\,\text{km}^2$), utilizing $12$ fire stations and $5,817$ nodes.
This reduced redundancy in the municipal core leads to a more conservative and localized assessment of resilience.
Furthermore, our flood model utilizes hydrodynamic stage data specific to the Trier gauge, ensuring higher fidelity for municipal planning despite the divergence from the regional benchmark.

\subsubsection{H1: Functional Gap Analysis}
\label{subsubsec:h1-analysis}
Hypothesis H1 is confirmed by a $1.10\,\%$ gap in the peak nadir between Level A ($65.50\,\%$) and Level C ($64.40\,\%$). While the difference in integrated resilience $RoA_{\text{Int}}$ appears small ($0.83\,\%$), this is due to the 336-hour window; the disruption is concentrated during the critical peak and recovery phases.
In these windows, the functional loss of decentralized fire stations due to utility exhaustion significantly hampers local response capacity.

\subsubsection{H2: Temporal Hysteresis and Recovery Lag}
\label{subsubsec:h2-analysis}
Hypothesis H2 is confirmed by the observed systemic recovery lag.
While Level A accessibility restores fully at hour 288 (end of recession), Level C remains compromised until hour 336.
This 48-hour recovery lag is driven by the internal reboot delays and hysteresis thresholds implemented in the FSM, reflecting the realistic challenges of site decontamination and system restarts post-inundation.

\subsection{Conclusion of Evaluation}
\label{subsec:evaluation-conclusion}

The results demonstrate that evaluating critical infrastructure through a single-infrastructure lens significantly underestimates the systemic risk.
The confirmation of H1 and H2 validates the necessity of compound multi-infrastructure modeling for urban disaster resilience planning.
In particular, the 48-hour recovery lag identified in Level C highlights a critical policy window for emergency logistics during the post-flood phase.
