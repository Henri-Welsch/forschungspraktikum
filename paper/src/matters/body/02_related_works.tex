Vorhandene Methoden zur Einschätzung der Resilienz kritischer Infrastrukturen berücksichtigen verschiedene Teilaspekte des Verhalten des Systems bei Störungen\cite{berdica2002,calvert2017,kaub2024,lickert2024}.

Existing methods for assessing the resilience of critical infrastructure consider various aspects of the system behavior during disruptions\cite{berdica2002,calvert2017,kaub2024,lickert2024}.

%Subsection 2.1 Resilienz und kritische Infrastrukturen


% Resilienz define

Resilience is not a uniformly defined concept. Calvert and Snelder define in their study as "the ability of a systems cope with disturbances and recover its original function after a loss of function"\cite{calvert2017}. Kaub et al. consider in terms of road networks the resilience as an ability to overcome disruptions as well as to maintain or regain its \textit{serviceability}\cite{kaub2024}.

Bruneau et al. views resilience as time-dependent, understanding the performance of a system as a time-dependent quantity\cite{bruneau2003}. A disruption can lead to reduced system performance, followed by a recover phase gradually retunrs to the original performance level\cite{bruneau2003}. Thus, resilience encompasses not only functional limitations but also the development and restoration of a system performance over time\cite{bruneau2003}.

For the purposes of this paper, resilience is understood as a time-dependent property of a system, the evaluation of disruptions related functional losses as well as the consequential effects on a system performance over a period of time are taken into account.

% Subsection 2.2 Kritische Infrastrukturen, Interdependenzen und kaskadierende Ausfälle
%-Eine Ableitung zu kaskadierende Ausfälle aufbauen (Laprie, Buldyrev, vielleicht lickert)



Critical infrastructures can be understood as complex, interconnected networks that provide essential services\cite{chiaradonna2007}. Because of this interconnectedness, it is relevant to take the interdependencies between individual infrastructure components need to taking into account in the analysis\cite{chiaradonna2007}.

An interdependency can be described as a reciprocal relationship between two infrastructures in which state of one infrastructure influences or is related to the state of the other\cite{laprie2007}. Such interdependencies can take various forms and may, for example, be physical or cyber-related in nature\cite{chiaradonna2007}.

An infrastructure failure that causes components of another infrastructure to fail is referred to as a cascading failure\cite{laprie2007}. Buldyrev et al. further demonstrate that such a failure process can continue recursively in interdependent networks. In this context, failures of nodes in one network can lead to failures in a dependent network, which in turn can cause further failures in the original network\cite{buldyrev2010}. This demonstrates that a disruption in a one network can develop into a cascade of failures\cite{buldyrev2010}.



%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Subsection 2.3 Zeitabhängige Funktionsfähigkeit und Lifetimes


Lickert et al. use a network model to investigate failure cascades caused by power outages\cite{lickert2024}. Various critical infrastructures and their interdependencies are represented in a network model\cite{lickert2024}. However, that does not necessarily mean that critical infrastructures will fail immediately\cite{lickert2024}. For example, hospitals are required to have emergency power generators\cite{lickert2024}.

However, critical infrastructures cannot remain operational indefinitely without external supply and lose their functionality after a certain period of time\cite{lickert2024}. Lickert et al. refer to this period as \textit{lifetimes}\cite{lickert2024}. In addition to \textit{lifetimes}, they also model \textit{repair times}\cite{lickert2024}. These describe the time required for a critical infrastructure to become operational again after the necessary supply capacity has been restored\cite{lickert2024}. Lickert et al. model functional states using a binary classification and distinguish between an \textit{operative} and an \textit{dysfunctional} state\cite{lickert2024}.

In addition to the functional aspect of critical infrastructure, its accessibility through the road network is also relevant in the event of disruptions\cite{lickert2024,kaub2024}. For instance, emergency services rely on existing road network\cite{kaub2024}.

Kaub et al. understand \textit{serviceability} as the availability of connections between two network nodes. These connections determine the distance and other costs of travel\cite{kaub2024}.

Disruptions can lead to a reduction in the \textit{serviceability} of a road network\cite{berdica2002}. According to Berdica, the vulnerability of a road traffic system is its susceptibility to events that can lead to a significant reduction in \textit{serviceability}\cite{berdica2002}.

Various aspects can be considered when assessing resilience, including the recovery time and the degree of \textit{serviceability}\cite{kaub2024}.

Calvert and Snelder developed the \textit{Link Performance Index for Resilience} (LPIR) as a method for assessing the resilience of individual road segments within the context of a larger road network\cite{calvert2017}. This index examines how well a road segment can withstand a disruption in traffic flow and how effectively it recovers afterward\cite{calvert2017}. However, the LPIR requires detailed data and is therefore less suitable for hypothetical scenarios\cite{kaub2024}. Even complete failures of road segments can be modeled to a limited extent using this method\cite{kaub2024}.

The \textit{Loss of Serviceability} (LoS) Index assesses resilience by comparing shortest paths between a start node and a destination node under normal disrupted network conditions\cite{kaub2024}.Impairments to \textit{serviceability} can lead to an increase in travel time or the distance traveled\cite{kaub2024}. This allows for an analysis of the detours caused by the failure of certain road sections\cite{kaub2024}. However, this comparison reaches its limit when one node is completely isolated from the rest of network\cite{kaub2024}. In this case, the distance will be considered as infinite, causing the index to lose its explanatory value\cite{kaub2024}.

% Subsection 2.5 Robustness of Acessibility (RoA)
%%%

Due to the limitations of the LoS Kaub et al. introduce \textit{Robustness of Accessibility} (RoA) as a metric for evaluating urban road networks\cite{kaub2024}. Compared to LoS, RoA does not focus individual road segments but rather the entire road network\cite{kaub2024}.

To calculate the RoA as defined by Kaub et al., the road network is represented as a graph\cite{kaub2024}. In this approach, an intact network is compared to a disrupted version of the same network based on the shortest paths\cite{kaub2024}. In addition, when comparing road network states, the RoA also takes into account individual inaccessible locations as well as parts of the network that remain usable\cite{kaub2024}.

The RoA value is then calculated based on the determined shortest paths\cite{kaub2024}. Here, $c(s_j,d_j)$ describes the length of the shortest path in the intact network, and $c'(s'_j,d'_j)$ describes the corresponding length in the disrupted network. For each selected pair, the ratio of these two path lengths is determined. The individual ratios are then weighted by $a_{C,j}$ and combined to form a final RoA value\cite{kaub2024}. The RoA is calculated as weighted sum of these ratios\cite{kaub2024}:

\begin{equation}
    \text{RoA}(\mathcal{C}) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \left( a_{\mathcal{C},j} \cdot \frac{c(s_j,d_j)}{c'(s'_j, d'_j)} \right)
\end{equation}

The RoA can take values between 0 and 1\cite{kaub2024}.A high value indicates a resilient raod network, while a low value indicates lower resilience\cite{kaub2024}. A value of 0 corresponds to a complete failure of all considered connections\cite{kaub2024}. If a route exists in the intact netowrk no longer exists in the disrupted network, the corresponding quotient is set to 0\cite{kaub2024}.

For the present paper, the approaches of Kaub et al. and Lickert et al. are particularly relevant, as they consider different aspects of the resilience of critical infrastructure. While Kaub et al. examine accessibility in disrupted road networks\cite{kaub2024}, they do not consider the functional aspect of facilities. Lickert et al. model the functional aspect as well as the cascading failures of interdependent networks, taking into account \textit{lifetimes} and \textit{repair times}\cite{lickert2024}.

These two approaches examine the aspect of road based accessibility and the time dependent functionality of critical infrastructure separately.
A combined assessment of both aspects is not provided by these two approaches.

Based on this, the following chapter presents an approach that extends the RoA with a time dependent functional aspect.
