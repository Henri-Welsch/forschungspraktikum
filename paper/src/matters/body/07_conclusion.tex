\section{Fazit und Ausblick}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% - Beantwortung der Forschungsfrage

This research extended the Reliability of Accessibility (RoA) index to account for the complex interdependencies between urban transportation and utility infrastructure during extreme flood events. By integrating a four-state finite state machine and a road-network-constrained infrastructure coupling (NCNN), we moved beyond simple physical inundation models to a more comprehensive functional resilience assessment.

Our findings from the case study in Trier demonstrate that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cascading failures significantly amplify accessibility loss}, often impacting areas geographically distant from the flood zone.
    \item \textbf{Internal facility buffers and recovery protocols (reboot delays)} are critical determinants of the total resilience, introducing significant temporal lags in service restoration.
    \item \textbf{A multi-tier evaluation approach} is essential for isolating the specific vulnerabilities of different utility networks and identifying compound risks.
\end{enumerate}

Future research should focus on incorporating dynamic utility routing, probabilistic failure modeling for infrastructure components, and the integration of real-time population movement data. Ultimately, this framework provides urban planners and emergency managers with a powerful tool for quantifying the resilience of critical services and prioritizing infrastructure hardening in an era of increasing climate-driven hazards.
